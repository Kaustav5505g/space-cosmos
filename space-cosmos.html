✦ Space Exploration

Major Space Missions

Era-defining journeys that pushed the boundary of human knowledge — from the first satellite to the edge of interstellar space.

USSR / Roscosmos1957

Sputnik 1

The world's first artificial satellite. A 58 cm aluminium sphere that transmitted radio pulses, Sputnik 1 inaugurated the Space Age and ignited the Space Race. It orbited Earth for 3 weeks before battery failure and re-entered in January 1958.

USSR / Roscosmos1961

Vostok 1 — Yuri Gagarin

On 12 April 1961, Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space, completing one orbit of Earth in 108 minutes. "Poyekhali!" (Let's go!) echoed around the world. He ejected and parachuted separately to the ground — standard procedure then.

NASA1969

Apollo 11

On 20 July 1969, Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed on the Sea of Tranquillity while Michael Collins orbited above. Armstrong's first words were heard by 600 million people. They collected 21.5 kg of moon rock and completed humanity's greatest adventure.

NASA1977

Voyager 1 & 2

Twin spacecraft now in interstellar space. Voyager 1 is over 23 billion km from Earth. Voyager 2 remains the only craft to visit Uranus and Neptune. Both carry a Golden Record — sounds and images of Earth for any extraterrestrial finder.

NASA1990

Hubble Space Telescope

Launched in 1990 and serviced 5 times by Space Shuttle crews, Hubble has made over 1.5 million observations. It revealed the universe's expansion rate, confirmed supermassive black holes, and revealed the deep cosmos with breathtaking clarity. Still operational after 34 years.

NASA / ESA / ASI1997

Cassini–Huygens

Orbited Saturn for 13 years, discovering that Enceladus has a subsurface ocean spraying geysers into space. The Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005 — the most distant soft landing ever. Cassini ended with a deliberate plunge into Saturn's atmosphere in 2017.

NASA2004

Spirit & Opportunity Rovers

Designed for 90 days, Opportunity operated 15 years driving 45 km across Mars — 60× its planned life. Together they found compelling evidence of ancient liquid water. Spirit discovered silica soils hinting at past hydrothermal activity before getting stuck in 2010.

New Horizons

First close flyby of Pluto in July 2015 after 9.5 years. Revealed Pluto as a geologically active world with water ice mountains, nitrogen glaciers in the heart-shaped Tombaugh Regio, and a thin atmosphere. Then flew by Kuiper Belt Object Arrokoth in 2019 — the most distant object ever visited.

Curiosity Rover

Nuclear-powered car-sized rover in Gale Crater since 2012. Confirmed Mars was once habitable, detecting organic molecules, methane spikes, and complex chemistry in ancient lake sediments. Climbed Mount Sharp revealing billions of years of Martian geological history. Still fully operational as of 2024.

Rosetta — Comet Landing

ESA chased Comet 67P for 10 years then deployed Philae — the first comet nucleus landing. Rosetta discovered organic molecules and found the comet's water isotopically different from Earth's oceans, rewriting theories on how Earth got its water. Mission ended in 2016 by crashing Rosetta into the comet.

Parker Solar Probe

The fastest human-made object ever — within 6.2 million km of the Sun. In 2021 it became the first spacecraft to physically enter the Sun's corona. Helping scientists understand why the corona is hotter than the surface and how the solar wind is accelerated to millions of km/h.

Perseverance hunts for ancient biosignatures in Jezero Crater and caches rock samples for future Earth return. Ingenuity became the first powered aircraft to fly on another planet — completing 70+ flights. India's Mars Orbiter Mission made ISRO the first Asian agency to reach Mars and the first in the world to succeed on the very first attempt. Reached Mars in September 2014 and operated for 8 years — far exceeding its 6-month design life — at a cost of just $74 million.

Falcon 9 Reusability

SpaceX revolutionised rocketry by landing and reusing orbital-class boosters. Falcon 9 boosters now routinely fly 20+ times, dramatically cutting launch costs. This sparked the commercial space revolution and made SpaceX the dominant global launch provider, changing the economics of access to space forever.

International Space Station

Continuously inhabited since November 2000 — over 23 years of unbroken human presence in space. It orbits at 28,000 km/h completing 16 orbits per day. Over 3,000 experiments have been conducted. The ISS is the size of a football field and humanity's most expensive structure at ~$150 billion.

DART — Asteroid Deflection

Humanity's first planetary defence test. DART crashed into asteroid Dimorphos at 22,000 km/h and successfully changed its orbital period by 32 minutes — far exceeding the minimum needed to count as a success. Proven: we have technology to deflect an asteroid threatening Earth if we detect it early enough.

✦ Milestones

Greatest Space Achievements

The breakthroughs that forever changed how we understand our place in the cosmos.

1969👟

First Steps on Another World

Six Apollo missions placed 12 astronauts on the Moon. They returned 382 kg of moon rock confirming the giant impact hypothesis of lunar formation and proving humans can live and work beyond Earth.

1990📸

Pale Blue Dot

On Valentine's Day 1990, Voyager 1 photographed Earth from 6 billion km away — a tiny dot in a sunbeam. Carl Sagan's reflection became one of the most profound meditations on humanity's cosmic humility.

1998🌌

Discovery of Dark Energy

Two teams studying distant supernovae found the universe's expansion is accelerating, implying a mysterious "dark energy" comprising 68% of all energy in existence. This earned the 2011 Nobel Prize in Physics.

2012🔬

Higgs Boson Confirmed

CERN's Large Hadron Collider confirmed the existence of the Higgs boson on 4 July 2012, completing the Standard Model of particle physics and explaining the origin of mass in the universe. Nobel Prize 2013.

2015〰️

Gravitational Waves Detected

LIGO detected spacetime ripples from merging black holes 1.3 billion light-years away — confirming Einstein's century-old prediction and opening an entirely new form of astronomy. Nobel Prize 2017.

2019🕳️

First Image of a Black Hole

The Event Horizon Telescope imaged M87* — a black hole 6.5 billion solar masses, 55 million light-years away. In 2022 they imaged Sagittarius A*, the 4-million solar mass black hole at our own galaxy's centre.

2020🚀

Commercial Human Spaceflight

SpaceX Crew Dragon became the first private spacecraft to carry NASA astronauts to the ISS, ending 9 years of Soyuz dependence. By 2021, the first all-civilian orbital mission (Inspiration4) launched without any professional astronauts.

2021🚁

First Powered Flight on Mars

Ingenuity helicopter achieved the first powered, controlled flight on another planet on 19 April 2021. Originally planned for 5 flights, it completed 70+, scouting terrain and proving aerial vehicles are viable on Mars.

2022🔭

JWST Deep Field

JWST's first deep field image showed thousands of galaxies as they existed 13 billion years ago in a patch of sky the size of a grain of sand. It discovered mature galaxies just 300 million years after the Big Bang — challenging all formation models.

2022☄️

Planetary Defence Proven

NASA's DART deflected asteroid Dimorphos by 32 minutes — 26× the minimum needed. The first demonstration that humanity has the technology to redirect an asteroid on a collision course with Earth if detected early.

2023🌙

India at the Lunar South Pole

Chandrayaan-3 landed at the Moon's south pole — a region of huge importance for future colonisation due to potential water ice. India became the 4th nation to achieve a soft lunar landing and the only one to reach the pole.

2024🌟

Starship — Fully Reusable Rocket

SpaceX's Starship — the most powerful rocket ever built — successfully completed integrated test flights, recovering both the Super Heavy booster and Starship upper stage. Designed to carry humans to the Moon and Mars, it marks the dawn of multi-planetary civilisation.

✦ Our Home Galaxy

The Milky Way

A barred spiral galaxy ~100,000 light-years across, home to 200–400 billion stars. Our solar system sits 26,000 light-years from the galactic centre in the Orion Arm — light from the core takes 26,000 years to reach us.

🕳️

Sagittarius A* — A supermassive black hole 4 million times the mass of the Sun at our galaxy's centre. First directly imaged by the Event Horizon Telescope in 2022.

⏱️

Galactic year — The Solar System takes 225–250 million years to orbit the galaxy once. Earth has completed ~20 galactic orbits since it formed 4.5 billion years ago.

🌌

Andromeda Collision — In ~4.5 billion years the Milky Way and Andromeda Galaxy will merge in a spectacular slow-motion collision spanning tens of millions of years.

🌑

Dark Matter Halo — An invisible dark matter halo ~10× the mass of all visible stars surrounds the Milky Way, holding it together with gravity we can measure but cannot directly see.

✦ Human Journey

Timeline of Discovery

1543

Copernican Revolution

Copernicus places the Sun at the centre of the solar system, launching the Scientific Revolution and shattering the Earth-centric worldview that had held for 1,400 years.

1687

Newton's Laws of Gravitation

Newton's Principia unifies terrestrial and celestial mechanics — explaining planetary orbits, tides, and the force that governs all motion in the cosmos.

1905

Special Relativity — E = mc²

Einstein reveals that mass and energy are equivalent and the speed of light is absolute — overturning centuries of classical mechanics and reshaping our understanding of space and time.

1929

Hubble Discovers Expanding Universe

Edwin Hubble observes that galaxies recede at speeds proportional to their distance, proving the universe began from a single point and is still expanding today.

1957

Space Age Begins — Sputnik

The Soviet Union launches Sputnik 1. Humanity first extends its reach beyond the atmosphere and the Space Race begins in earnest.

1969

First Moon Landing

Apollo 11 lands on the Moon. Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walk on another world, watched by 600 million people — the most ambitious thing humanity has ever achieved.

1990

Hubble Space Telescope Launches

Hubble transforms astronomy, measuring the universe's age, confirming dark energy and black holes, and delivering the most beautiful images of the cosmos ever taken.

1998

Cosmic Expansion is Accelerating

Supernova observations reveal the universe's expansion is speeding up, implying a mysterious dark energy we still cannot explain makes up 68% of all energy in existence.

2015

Gravitational Waves Detected

LIGO detects spacetime ripples from merging black holes, confirming Einstein's last untested major prediction and opening gravitational wave astronomy as a new field.

2019

First Black Hole Image

The Event Horizon Telescope reveals M87*, a 6.5-billion solar-mass black hole 55 million light-years away, matching Einstein's predictions with extraordinary precision.

2021

James Webb Telescope Launches

JWST launches on Christmas Day, designed to see 13.6 billion years into the past, revealing the first stars and galaxies that lit up after the Big Bang's darkness.

2023

India at the Lunar South Pole

Chandrayaan-3 makes India the first nation to land near the lunar south pole — the richest target for future human settlement due to permanently shadowed water ice deposits.
